Alright everyone, let's get started. Today we are talking about neural networks and how they learn. A neural network is made of layers of neurons, and each neuron computes a weighted sum of its inputs. The weighted sum is passed through an activation function, which adds non-linearity to the network. Without activation functions, stacking layers would just give you one big linear model. Common activation functions are ReLU, sigmoid, and tanh, and ReLU is the most popular today. Quick reminder before I forget, your midterm exam is on Thursday, October 29 and it covers chapters 3 through 5. Okay so how does a network actually learn? It learns through gradient descent. Gradient descent adjusts the weights a little bit at a time to reduce the loss function. The loss function measures how wrong the network's predictions are compared to the true labels. The learning rate controls how big each step is, and if it is too large the training will diverge. Backpropagation is the algorithm that computes the gradient for every weight using the chain rule.
